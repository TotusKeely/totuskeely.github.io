CASE STUDY 01 · MECHANICAL DESIGN

Industrial Crossdraft Spray Booth

A large-scale equipment-design project requiring structural framing, sheet-metal enclosures, filtration, access, lighting integration, purchased components and manufacturing documentation.

8assemblies
~100total parts
~10design changes

My contribution

  • Developed most of the mechanical design using SolidWorks large assemblies, sheet metal, weldments and envelope-based modeling.
  • Produced manufacturing and assembly drawings, flat patterns, DXFs, cut lists and multiple BOM types.
  • Evaluated filter velocity, pressure drop, concrete-anchor strength, door-hinge strength, access, fastening and manufacturability.
  • Designed wall assemblies around approximately 1 ft of clearance along the sides and rear, requiring piece-by-piece installation from inside the booth.
  • Presented design iterations to lead engineers and floor managers and incorporated feedback into the final system.

Installation constraint

The booth had to fit within an existing space with approximately 1 ft of clearance along the sides and rear. The front and top remained accessible, but limited exterior access meant the wall system had to be designed for piece-by-piece assembly from inside the booth.

Design validation

Engineering checks included filter velocity, pressure drop, concrete-anchor strength and door-hinge strength. Design reviews with lead engineers and floor managers resulted in approximately 10 incorporated changes.

CASE STUDY 02 · MECHATRONICS

Autonomous Quadruped Navigation Robot

A semester-long project to guide a 12-DOF quadruped from one corner of an arena to another while detecting and avoiding obstacles.

Two-person team project · Autonomous navigation, sensing and embedded-system integration

12degrees of freedom
Pi 5navigation computer
UltrasonicSensor

Engineering approach

  • Integrated external sensing and navigation logic on a Raspberry Pi 5 while using the robot's existing ESP32-based gait control.
  • Replaced an impractical LiDAR-and-magnetometer concept with a servo-mounted ultrasonic sensor that sweeps the robot's surroundings.
  • The navigation logic used obstacle avoidance, timed wall detection, wall following and a two-stage path to reach the target corner.
  • Testing addressed Raspberry Pi-to-ESP32 communication, sensor behavior, battery limitations and repeated iterations.

Key engineering decision

When LiDAR and magnetometer integration proved impractical on the Raspberry Pi, the sensing architecture was revised to a servo-mounted ultrasonic sensor and the navigation strategy was reworked around obstacle avoidance, wall detection and wall following.

CASE STUDY 03 · ROBOTICS & AUTOMATION

6-DOF Industrial Robot Pick-and-Place

A RoboDK simulation of a HIWIN RA605-710-GB robot and RobotiQ 2F-85 gripper completing a repeatable three-point pick-and-place sequence.

Two-person team · Primary focus: Python programming · Shared work: simulation development, motion testing and validation

6robot axes
4×4homogeneous transforms
3pick-and-place points

Technical work

  • Led development of the Python program used to control the simulated sequence and output transformation data.
  • Collaboratively defined robot, tool and target coordinate frames for the simulated workcell.
  • Applied forward and inverse kinematics, motion planning and collision checking while developing the motion sequence.
  • Verified transformation matrices using orthogonality and determinant checks before validating the final sequence.

Validation and motion revision

Python-generated transformation matrices were checked by hand using rotation-matrix orthogonality and determinant conditions. When direct joint motion created collision risk, an intermediate target was added to route the robot clear of the pedestal before continuing the sequence.
